**Thievery**

1\.      **Issue 1\.** Why does thievery not work in its current state?

Thievery as of the current season does not feel good for either party of the thievery, dependent on each chance of thievery. A thief either gets dirt and scraps for a hefty price of steel and over an hour of waiting for stealing timers.

On the other hand, they can also just hit the jackpot, but the issue is that the jackpot is not out of nowhere, but rather on someone else’s behalf. Which does not feel good for the person being stolen from. Both scenarios end up with a party not feeling good about it.

Furthermore, thieving being completely random makes it nearly impossible to make a good storyline out of said thieveries. If a thief wants to make a proper storyline, they usually need to have a say in what they steal, which you don’t have as of right now.

2\.      **Issue 2\.** The previous iteration of thievery let the thief control what was stolen with some regulation. This meant they could make a storyline easier out of the thievery, and they did not need to succumb to the randomness of theft. Why did this not work?

The problem with the previous iteration was not really the thieving itself, but rather that it required too much goodwill that the thief in question would do it in good faith. Good faith here was something which frequently became a question in the air, as can be interpreted in different ways. Like if a thief stole most of your valuables, but left clues like a treasure hunt? It did not feel good, even if there was a proper storyline in place, and the thief obviously made it easy to get items back. A day or two of going back to square one of the season.

As it all became a question of what “good faith” is, it created issues from a moderation perspective on the staff. Or bad faith actors misused the system.

3\.      **Solution.** Is there any system that can fix both issues? Remove the complete randomness, as well as making sure staff don’t constantly have to regulate the definition of “goodwill”, and if the thief in question had a misunderstanding, or was a bad actor in secret.

A weighted stealing system where the thief gets a partial say in what they get to steal. Thieves will get a menu like the profession’s menu, where they can pick and choose certain categories of items they want to steal from. A thief will get a limited amount of “thievery points”, where they can allocate what they want to steal. These categories will be weighted in quality and quantity of goods within said category. So mithril minerals might cost 5 points, while abyss minerals cost 3 points, as an example. With the thief having a maximum allocation of 25 points they must choose wisely. This determination of category allows the thief to see those goods when stealing. There will also be another weight tied to the items themselves, which will determine how much a thief will be able to steal. Example: A thief is allowed to steal 100 weights of items. Mythril ingots are 5 weights. That means the thief can steal 20 mythril ingots from said chest but would not be able to steal anything else. Tier V food might have a weight of 2, meaning that the thief might want to steal a bit of both. A 50/50 split would be 10 mythril (5 weight x 10\) and 25 tier V food (2 weight x 25), which would equal 100 total weights from said chest. And since all goods and categories have different weights, this can be balanced and changed throughout the season, making it more accessible to balance thievery.

 

4\.      **Other changes.** Another issue with thievery is the staleness of waiting for lockpicks. As well as other minor issues.

Having to sit and wait for lockpicks to work on a random change can be tedious with bad luck. Hence it has now changed to a more interactive gameplay system, where the thief must succeed in a minigame instead. The minigame will give a penalty if the thief spams it, making it harder if the thief is in a rush.

Furthermore, there will be new tiers of lockpicks and keys which will influence the minigames’ difficulty, meaning that certain robberies can be hard committed, and doors can be defended better. For now it has been discussed of key qualities of tier 1 to 5, and of lockpicks tier 0 to 5\. (Tier 0 lockpicks is made of nothing, meant for prison escape rp, making the minigame real difficult, but giving a chance of escape. Tier 1 is iron, Tier 2 diamonds, Tier 3 steel, Tier 4 abyss, Tier 5 mythril.)

**Banditry**

1\.      When banditry was first introduced this season, bandits misused the ability to steal items from players, hence it was removed, only being able to steal pouch gold. This made banditry kind of pointless as most did not carry money around on a regular basis. So, what can be done to change this?

Banditry will also be able to choose stealable items similar to a thief and will be able to open someone’s inventory to see items dependent on their thievery point unlocks. From there they will be able to steal based on what they see.

**Assasin**

Assassination contracts have had issues with regulations of what is a justifiable assassination? Is simply dislike a person enough for an assassination? Or must the reason be deeper?

 

Another issue is also the amount of work staff needs to do in tickets for assassinations, as it has always been a case-by-case basis.

 

There have also been irregularities in needed pay from ooc friendships coming into play, friends giving bargain assassinations, or free of charge. Or simply asking to do assassinations for themselves.

 

To solve these issues, assassinations will be moved to a contract-based system, with a money sink system built into it, to deter players from doing assassinations for minor grudges. Someone looking to hire an assassin will post a contract on a public assassin board where assassins can make offers for said contract. It will cost an initial fee to post the contract to the assassins, and the assassins can give their price for their job in their offer. The initial fee also covers for friendship contracts, and makes people unable to do contracts for themselves.
